\title{Propagated class exclusion for direct semi-supervised non-negative matrix factorization}

\author{Deshu Sun}
\author{Feng Wang\corref{cor1}}
\cortext[cor1]{Corresponding author.}
\ead{wangfeng@gzmu.edu.cn}
\address{College of Data Science and Information Engineering, Guizhou Minzu University, Guiyang, Guizhou 550025, P.R. China}

\begin{abstract}
Semi-supervised non-negative matrix factorization (NMF) commonly uses
labels to shape a representation before a separate class decision.
We formulate propagated class-exclusion NMF (PCE-NMF), in which each
factor component denotes a class and graph-derived harmonic evidence
penalizes unsupported class contributions. Labeled samples retain
exact class support, and a scale-invariant contribution energy assigns
unlabeled samples directly from the factors. We evaluate the method
with 20 paired splits on ten data sets against eight established
methods and two mechanism controls. PCE-NMF exceeds a matched
hard-exclusion factorization on all ten data sets, isolating the value
of propagated exclusion within the factorization. Its mean accuracy
difference from harmonic prediction alone is $-0.0006$, with
data-set-level gains and losses.
PCE-NMF attains the
highest accuracy among PCE-NMF, HLPNMF, and GOCNMF on COIL20, ORL, and
COIL100, while HLPNMF leads on six data sets. A no-cache timing study
places PCE-NMF between GOCNMF and HLPNMF on every data set. The study
establishes a direct class-aware factorization and separates the
contribution of propagated exclusion from the predictive strength of
the underlying harmonic field.  The source code is publicly available at https://github.com/matrix-research-lab-Wf/PCE-NMF.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Non-negative matrix factorization represents a non-negative data matrix
through a small set of non-negative factors~\cite{lee1999parts,lee2000algorithms}. In clustering, the sample
factor indicates how strongly each sample is associated with each
component. NMF has been used for document clustering~\cite{xu2003document};
its connections to spectral and orthogonal clustering formulations
have also been studied~\cite{ding2005spectral,ding2006orthogonal}.
Sparsity constraints~\cite{hoyer2004sparse} and alternative numerical
solvers~\cite{lin2007projected,kim2011fast} address different aspects
of the factorization. Limited class labels can make components easier
to interpret: they may shape a representation~\cite{liu2012cnmf},
constrain labeled coordinates, or propagate class information to
unlabeled samples~\cite{zhu2003harmonic}.

The class meaning of an NMF component is usually assigned after the
factorization. With a small labeled set, it can instead be specified
during fitting. Each of the $c$ components then denotes one class, and
the labeled rows of the sample factor have exact class support. For an
unlabeled sample, support alone leaves the relative cost of candidate
classes unspecified. A harmonic field on the sample graph provides
class evidence at these unlabeled vertices.

PCE-NMF uses the complement of that evidence as a class-specific
penalty on each factor contribution. The same contribution energy is
used for the final class decision. This gives the graph evidence a
specific role in the factorization while preserving ordinary NMF
reconstruction. The construction also makes the decision invariant to
positive diagonal rescaling of the two factors.

We examine three questions. How much does propagated exclusion change
a hard-support factorization? How do factor-based decisions compare
with predictions from the harmonic field itself? How does the method
compare with established semi-supervised NMF methods under matched
splits and native prediction rules? Ten-data-set experiments address
these questions; additional fixed experiments measure sensitivity to
label fraction, corruption, class count, runtime, and numerical
convergence. The results show large gains over hard exclusion within
the factorization, similar mean ACC to harmonic prediction, and strong
performance on COIL20 and COIL100.

The paper makes three contributions. First, it defines a class-wise
exclusion term on factor contribution energy and an aligned direct
decision rule. Second, it establishes well-posedness of the regularized
harmonic step, descent of the exact alternating updates under stated
conditions, and scale invariance of class energy. Third, matched
controls isolate the contribution of propagated exclusion and show
that the resulting factor-based decisions have data-set-specific gains
and losses relative to direct harmonic prediction. A nine-method
benchmark and no-cache timing study characterize the predictive and
computational behavior of the construction.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Representation-level semi-supervision in NMF}

Constrained NMF incorporates partial labels into the factorization to
learn a discriminative representation~\cite{liu2012cnmf}. Other
representation-level methods use discriminative terms on labeled
samples~\cite{xing2021dssnmf,li2025ednmf}, local constraints
~\cite{shen2026lcnmf}, or a combination of label discrimination and
graph regularization~\cite{xing2021gnmfld}. The learned coordinates
can then support a downstream class decision. PCE-NMF uses
$c$ components with explicit class meanings and restricts the labeled
rows of $V$ to their known class support. Its unlabeled penalty acts on
the contribution of each class component to each sample, so the class
decision is obtained from the factors themselves.

\subsection{Graph and propagation mechanisms}

Graph-based clustering includes normalized cuts~\cite{shi2000ncut},
Laplacian embeddings~\cite{belkin2003laplacian}, and spectral
clustering~\cite{luxburg2007tutorial}. Graph-regularized NMF
preserves local relations in the low-dimensional
representation~\cite{cai2011gnmf}; symmetric NMF provides another
factorization of a sample affinity matrix~\cite{kuang2012symmetric}.
Harmonic label propagation solves a graph boundary-value problem to
extend labels from observed to unobserved vertices~\cite{zhu2003harmonic},
while local and global consistency uses a graph-smoothing
formulation~\cite{zhou2003consistency}.

In semi-supervised NMF, pairwise constraints can modify the graph
~\cite{wang2016constraint}. Label and constraint propagation have also
been combined in the factorization~\cite{mo2024propagation}; hypergraph
methods use higher-order sample relations~\cite{yin2023hypergraph},
and pivotal-aware models propagate both point labels and pairwise
constraints~\cite{yang2025pivotal}. Weighted label propagation is
coupled to NMF in SNMFWLP~\cite{jing2026snmfwlp}. HLPNMF couples
high-order propagation with NMF through a hypergraph-induced
transition structure~\cite{li2026hlpnmf}. PCE-NMF computes a harmonic class field
first and uses it as fixed class-exclusion evidence during
factorization. Thus its graph affects the class penalties, while the
reconstruction remains the ordinary two-factor NMF term.

\subsection{Direct class-semantic factorization}

GOCNMF embeds one-hot labels in the representation of labeled samples
and applies graph regularization to guide unlabeled
representations~\cite{li2025gocnmf}. PCE-NMF also assigns class
meanings to factor coordinates, but it uses hard class support rather
than fixing labeled coordinates to one-hot values. For unlabeled
samples, it penalizes class-specific reconstruction energy according
to harmonic evidence. This separates the observed label constraint
from the propagated preference over candidate classes.
Robust semi-supervised NMF~\cite{peng2021robust} and self-supervised
semi-supervised NMF~\cite{chavoshinejad2023s4} pursue complementary
goals through robust losses or additional self-supervisory signals.

\section{Propagated Class-Exclusion NMF}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Problem formulation}

Let $X=[x_1,\ldots,x_n]\in\R_+^{d\times n}$ contain $n$ non-negative
samples with $d$ features, and let $c$ be the number of classes. We
factorize $X\approx UV^\top$ with
$U=[u_1,\ldots,u_c]\in\R_+^{d\times c}$ and
$V=(v_{ik})\in\R_+^{n\times c}$. A labeled index set
$\mathcal L\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}$ supplies labels
$y_i\in\{1,\ldots,c\}$ for $i\in\mathcal L$. The class identity of
component $k$ is fixed by these labels. Nonzero samples are normalized
to unit Euclidean norm before graph construction and factorization.

\subsection{Harmonic class evidence}

Construct a binary $p$-nearest-neighbor graph from Euclidean distances
between the normalized samples and symmetrize its adjacency matrix
$W\in\{0,1\}^{n\times n}$. Let
$D=\operatorname{diag}(W\ones)$ and $L=D-W$. For each connected
component containing both labeled and unlabeled vertices, partition
its Laplacian by the labeled and unlabeled indices. The labeled
one-hot matrix $C$ defines the regularized harmonic field
\begin{equation}
  (L_{\mathcal U\mathcal U}+\eps I)F_{\mathcal U}
   =W_{\mathcal U\mathcal L}C,
  \qquad \eps=10^{-12}.
  \label{eq:harmonic_solver}
\end{equation}
The implementation clips negative numerical entries to zero and
normalizes each nonzero row to sum to one. A component without labeled
vertices contributes no propagated exclusion. Let $P\in[0,1]^{n\times c}$
denote the resulting class evidence on anchored unlabeled vertices;
the known one-hot labels are retained on $\mathcal L$.

\subsection{Propagated exclusion model}

Define the exclusion weights $Q\in[0,1]^{n\times c}$ by
\begin{equation}
Q_{ik}=\begin{cases}
0, & i\in\mathcal L,\ k=y_i,\\
1, & i\in\mathcal L,\ k\ne y_i,\\
1-P_{ik}, & i\notin\mathcal L\text{ in an anchored component},\\
0, & i\notin\mathcal L\text{ in an unanchored component}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:exclusion_weights}
\end{equation}
The PCE-NMF objective is
\begin{equation}
\min_{U,V\ge0}\quad
\mathcal J(U,V)=\|X-UV^\top\|_F^2
+\lambda\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{c}
 Q_{ik}\|u_k\|_2^2v_{ik}^2,
\label{eq:pce_objective}
\end{equation}
Here $\lambda\ge0$ controls exclusion strength. The weight
$1-P_{ik}$ penalizes a class contribution more strongly when the graph
provides less evidence for that class. The hard labeled-support
constraint $v_{ik}=0$ for $i\in\mathcal L$ and $k\ne y_i$ remains exact
throughout optimization.

\subsection{Direct clustering rule}

After optimization, sample $i$ receives the class
\begin{equation}
  \hat y_i=\argmaxop_{1\le k\le c}
  s_{ik},\qquad s_{ik}=\|u_k\|_2^2v_{ik}^2.
  \label{eq:class_energy}
\end{equation}
This rule uses the class meaning of the factors directly and assigns
samples from $V$ in one step.
Figure~\ref{fig:pce_mechanism} summarizes the path from labeled samples
through graph-derived exclusion to the class decision.

\subsection{Optimization}

Write $a_k=\|u_k\|_2^2$ and
$b_k=\sum_iQ_{ik}v_{ik}^2$. The gradients of the objective are
\begin{align}
\nabla_U\mathcal J
 &=2\bigl(UV^\top V-XV+\lambda U\operatorname{diag}(b)\bigr),
 \label{eq:grad_u}\\
\nabla_V\mathcal J
 &=2\bigl(VU^\top U-X^\top U
   +\lambda(Q\had V)\operatorname{diag}(a)\bigr).
 \label{eq:grad_v}
\end{align}
The implemented alternating multiplicative updates are
\begin{align}
U&\leftarrow U\had
 \frac{XV}{UV^\top V+\lambda U\operatorname{diag}(b)},
 \label{eq:update_u}\\
V&\leftarrow V\had
 \frac{X^\top U}{VU^\top U+\lambda(Q\had V)\operatorname{diag}(a)}.
 \label{eq:update_v}
\end{align}
The fractions are elementwise. Denominators are bounded below by
$10^{-12}$ in the MATLAB implementation. After each update, forbidden
labeled coordinates are reset to zero. The formal protocol uses 50
alternating updates and the parameters locked before evaluation.

\paragraph{Algorithm 1: PCE-NMF optimization.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Input:} Non-negative data $X$, labeled indices $\mathcal L$ and labels $y_i$, class count $c$, graph size $p$, locked penalty $\lambda$, and seed. Set $T=50$.
\item Normalize nonzero columns of $X$; construct the symmetric binary $p$-nearest-neighbor graph $W$. In each anchored component solve Eq.~\eqref{eq:harmonic_solver}, normalize its nonzero rows, and form $Q$ by Eq.~\eqref{eq:exclusion_weights}.
\item Initialize non-negative $U$ and $V$ from the recorded seed. Set $v_{ik}=0$ for labeled $i$ and $k\ne y_i$.
\item For $t=1,\ldots,T$, update $U$ and then $V$ by Eqs.~\eqref{eq:update_u}--\eqref{eq:update_v}; bound denominators below by $10^{-12}$ and reset forbidden labeled entries of $V$ to zero.
\item \textbf{Output:} Factors $U,V$ and unlabeled assignments from Eq.~\eqref{eq:class_energy}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Computational complexity}

The blocked distance calculation for the graph requires
$O(dn^2)$ arithmetic; selecting neighbors by sorting each distance
column adds $O(n^2\log n)$ comparisons. The symmetric binary graph
contains $O(np)$ edges. The harmonic step solves one sparse system per
anchored connected component; its time and memory depend on sparse
factorization fill-in. Each factor update costs
$O(dnc+(d+n)c^2)$ arithmetic and stores $O((d+n)c)$ factor entries,
in addition to $X$ and the graph. These bounds describe the
implementation's main operations; measured wall times are reported in
Section~\ref{sec:experiments}.

\section{Theoretical Analysis}
\label{sec:theory}

The descent proof uses the auxiliary-function approach for
multiplicative NMF updates~\cite{lee2000algorithms,li2026hlpnmf} and
accounts for the class-exclusion terms in both factor blocks.

\begin{proposition}[Well-posed regularized harmonic step]
For an undirected graph with non-negative weights and $\eps>0$, the
matrix $L_{\mathcal U\mathcal U}+\eps I$ in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:harmonic_solver} is symmetric positive definite. Hence
the regularized harmonic field is uniquely defined for every right-hand
side.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The graph Laplacian is symmetric positive semidefinite because
\begin{equation*}
z^\top Lz=\tfrac12\sum_{i,j}W_{ij}(z_i-z_j)^2\ge0
\end{equation*}
for every $z\in\R^n$. A principal submatrix of a positive semidefinite matrix
is positive semidefinite. Therefore, for nonzero
$h\in\R^{|\mathcal U|}$,
$h^\top(L_{\mathcal U\mathcal U}+\eps I)h
\ge\eps\|h\|_2^2>0$. Symmetric positive definiteness gives
invertibility and a unique solution.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Descent of the exact alternating updates]
\label{thm:exact_descent}
Fix $Q\in[0,1]^{n\times c}$, let $X\in\R_+^{d\times n}$, and take
$\lambda\ge0$. Apply Eqs.~\eqref{eq:update_u}--\eqref{eq:update_v}
in exact arithmetic, keeping forbidden labeled coordinates at zero.
At each update, assume every positive, permitted coordinate has a
positive denominator; any coordinate that reaches zero remains zero.
Each factor update then does not increase $\mathcal J$. Consequently,
the exact alternating sequence has nonincreasing objective values that
converge to a finite limit.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For fixed $V$, let $u\in\R_+^c$ be one row of $U$ and $x$ the
corresponding row of $X$. Up to a constant, its objective is
$f(u)=u^\top A u-2h^\top u$, where
$A=V^\top V+\lambda\operatorname{diag}(b)$ and $h=xV$.
For fixed $U$, the corresponding expression for row $v_i$ of $V$ has
$A_i=U^\top U+\lambda\operatorname{diag}(Q_{i:}\had a)$ and
$h_i=x_i^\top U$, where $x_i$ is column $i$ of $X$.
Both versions of $A$ are symmetric and entrywise nonnegative, and
their corresponding $h$ are nonnegative.
Restrict a row to its positive, permitted coordinates, write the
current vector as $z>0$, and set
$D=\operatorname{diag}((Az)_k/z_k)$. For any $\delta$,
\begin{equation*}
\delta^\top(D-A)\delta
=\tfrac12\sum_{j,k}A_{jk}z_jz_k
\left(\frac{\delta_j}{z_j}-\frac{\delta_k}{z_k}\right)^2\ge0.
\end{equation*}
Hence $g(z+\delta\mid z)=f(z)+2(Az-h)^\top\delta+\delta^\top D\delta$
majorizes $f(z+\delta)$ and is equal to it at $\delta=0$.
The positive diagonal matrix $D$ gives the nonnegative minimizer
$z^+=D^{-1}h=z\had h/(Az)$, which is the multiplicative update on
these coordinates. Thus
$f(z^+)\le g(z^+\mid z)\le g(z\mid z)=f(z)$.
If a numerator vanishes, the updated coordinate becomes zero and is
held there; the same argument applies to the remaining active
coordinates. Summing over rows proves descent for each factor block.
Finally, $\mathcal J\ge0$, so its sequence of objective values
converges to a finite limit.
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:exact_descent} establishes convergence of objective
values for the exact updates. The MATLAB implementation bounds
denominators below by $10^{-12}$ and clips factor entries to that
level; its objective trajectories are measured in
Section~\ref{sec:experiments}.

\begin{proposition}[Scale invariance of class energy]
For any positive diagonal matrix
$T=\operatorname{diag}(t_1,\ldots,t_c)$, the feasible transformation
$U\mapsto UT$ and $V\mapsto VT^{-1}$ leaves the reconstruction,
the exclusion penalty, and every class score in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:class_energy} unchanged.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Since $T$ is diagonal and positive,
$(UT)(VT^{-1})^\top=UV^\top$. For each class $k$,
$\|t_ku_k\|_2^2(v_{ik}/t_k)^2
=\|u_k\|_2^2v_{ik}^2$. This identity applies to each term of
the exclusion penalty and to each class score. Multiplication by a
positive diagonal matrix also preserves non-negativity and every
forbidden zero in $V$.
\end{proof}

For an anchored unlabeled sample, $Q_{ik}=1-P_{ik}$ gives the exact
identity
\begin{equation}
\sum_kQ_{ik}s_{ik}=\sum_ks_{ik}-\sum_kP_{ik}s_{ik}.
\label{eq:evidence_decomposition}
\end{equation}
The penalty therefore reduces the cost of contributions aligned with
the propagated evidence, relative to contributions to unsupported
classes. Equation~\eqref{eq:evidence_decomposition} is an algebraic
interpretation of the fixed objective; the measured effect of this
mechanism is tested by the matched controls in
Section~\ref{sec:experiments}.

\subsection{Experimental settings}

We evaluated NMF, CNMF, GNMFLD, EDNMF, LCNMF, GOCNMF, SNMFWLP,
HLPNMF, and PCE-NMF on YaleB, COIL20, Optdigits, ORL, PIE, COIL100,
AR, MSRA25, MNIST, and USPS. COIL20 and COIL100 originate from the
Columbia Object Image Library~\cite{nene1996coil20,nene1996coil}; MNIST is the
handwritten-digit benchmark described by LeCun et al.~\cite{lecun1998gradient}.
These ten data sets were fixed before the formal comparison and cover
face, object, and digit recognition tasks.
Nonzero samples were normalized to unit Euclidean
norm. For each class, $\max\{1,\lfloor 0.1n_k\rfloor\}$ samples were
labeled, where $n_k$ is the class size. All methods received the same
labeled split in each of 20 paired runs. Accuracy
(ACC), normalized mutual information (NMI), purity, F-score, precision,
and recall were measured on unlabeled samples. The external methods
retained their native training and prediction rules. Parameters were
fixed before the formal runs using source settings where available and
separate development splits for PCE-NMF.

\subsection{Paired statistical analysis}

For each data set, we first averaged each method over the same 20
seeds. Across-data-set comparisons then used the ten data-set means as
paired units, following the distinction between repeated trials and
independent data sets in multi-algorithm comparisons~\cite{demsar2006comparisons}.
We used the Friedman rank test~\cite{friedman1937ranks} for the
multi-method comparison and the paired Wilcoxon test~\cite{wilcoxon1945ranking}
with Holm adjustment~\cite{holm1979sequential} for the selected
pairwise comparisons. The mean PCE-NMF minus HLPNMF ACC difference was 0.0031
(four wins in ten data sets; Holm-adjusted Wilcoxon $p=0.9219$). Against
GOCNMF, the difference was 0.0150 (seven wins; adjusted $p=0.4648$).
The corresponding NMI differences were $-0.0050$ and 0.0121, with
adjusted $p=0.5508$ and $p=0.8457$. The three methods have
data-dependent performance profiles. Figure~\ref{fig:average_ranks}

\subsection{Controlled mechanism comparison}

We compared PCE-NMF with HCE-NMF, which retains NMF and the labeled
support constraint but removes propagated exclusion, and with a
harmonic-propagation-only predictor using the same graph field.
PCE-NMF exceeded HCE-NMF in ACC on all ten data sets. The mean
data-set-level difference was 0.4319 (95\% confidence interval
0.3124--0.5514; Holm-adjusted $p=0.0039$). Against harmonic
propagation alone, its mean difference was $-0.0006$ (95\% confidence
interval $-0.0134$--0.0121; adjusted $p=0.6953$). Propagated
exclusion therefore contributed substantially relative to hard
exclusion in this controlled comparison.

Table~\ref{tab:mechanism_acc} resolves the aggregate comparison by
data set. The factor-based rule exceeds harmonic prediction on COIL100
by 0.0243 and on COIL20 by 0.0143, whereas harmonic prediction exceeds
it on YaleB by 0.0428 and on PIE by 0.0113. Thus the two decisions
respond differently to the same graph evidence, even though their
mean ACC across the ten data sets is close.

\section{Discussion}

The controls measure two effects separately. The large difference from
HCE-NMF shows the contribution of propagated exclusion within the
factorization. Direct harmonic prediction has nearly the same mean ACC
as PCE-NMF across the ten data sets. The factor-based rule gains on
COIL20 and COIL100 and loses on YaleB, locating its empirical effect at
the data-set level. The harmonic field supplies a strong direct
predictor as well as the exclusion weights used in PCE-NMF.

PCE-NMF retains a non-negative class-aware representation and an
explicit reconstruction term alongside direct class decisions.
HLPNMF achieves higher ACC on several data sets, whereas PCE-NMF is
faster in the measured no-cache pipeline. The graph construction is
quadratic in sample count before neighbor selection, so the measured
runtime comparison includes this graph cost. The class-count experiment characterizes the tested COIL100
subsets; its accuracy ordering is specific to those subsets.

\section{Conclusion}

PCE-NMF turns harmonic class evidence into penalties on class-wise NMF
contributions and uses those contributions for direct decisions. Its
class-energy rule is invariant to factor rescaling. Across ten data
sets, propagated exclusion improved on a matched hard-support model.
Against direct harmonic prediction, its mean ACC difference was
$-0.0006$, with gains and losses across individual data sets. PCE-NMF performed
strongly on COIL20 and COIL100 and ran faster than HLPNMF, while
HLPNMF achieved higher ACC on several other data sets. The combined
results define the method's mathematical construction and its measured
performance profile under matched semi-supervised evaluation.
